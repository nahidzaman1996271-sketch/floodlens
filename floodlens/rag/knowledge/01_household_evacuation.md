# Household Flood Evacuation Guidance

> FloodLens-authored guidance summary for alert drafting. Not an official government document; always follow instructions from the Union Disaster Management Committee (UDMC) and local authorities.

## When to move

Leave for the assigned shelter as soon as an alert says your home is at CRITICAL risk or water is expected within 10 hours. Do not wait for water to enter the house: village roads are usually cut off before homesteads flood, and moving at night through moving water is the most common cause of drowning during floods.

If the risk level is HIGH, pack an emergency bag and agree as a family where to meet. If the level is WATCH, keep a phone charged and listen to the mosque loudspeaker, community radio and volunteer announcements.

## Who moves first

Move children, older people, pregnant women and people with disabilities first, while roads and boats are still available. One adult should stay with them at the shelter. Able-bodied adults can return to secure the house only if the alert time allows and the route is still dry.

## What to carry

Carry national ID cards, land and school documents in a sealed plastic bag, all prescription medicine, oral rehydration salts, drinking water, dry food such as chira and muri, a torch, a charged phone and some cash. Do not try to carry heavy furniture.

## Safety on the way

Never walk or drive through water deeper than knee height or water that is moving. Fifteen centimetres of moving water can knock an adult down. Keep away from electric poles and fallen wires. If you are trapped, go to the highest floor or roof and signal with a cloth or torch.
