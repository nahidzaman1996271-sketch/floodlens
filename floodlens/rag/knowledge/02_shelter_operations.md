# Flood Shelter Operations

> FloodLens-authored guidance summary. Not an official government document.

## Opening a shelter

Flood shelters in riverine and haor areas are usually school buildings or purpose-built multi-purpose shelters on raised plinths. The shelter committee should open the building, check that toilets and tube wells are usable, and register every arriving family by name, village and number of people as soon as a danger-level forecast is issued.

## Capacity and overflow

Each shelter has a planned capacity. When registrations reach about 80 percent of capacity, the shelter manager should tell the UDMC so that new arrivals can be directed to the next nearest shelter. Alerts should name a second shelter when the nearest one is close to full.

## Separate spaces

Keep a separate, private area for women and adolescent girls, a space for breastfeeding mothers, and a corner for people who are sick. Livestock should be kept on the raised ground outside the shelter, never inside the living area.

## Water and health

Drinking water must come from a raised tube well or be treated with purification tablets. Flood water spreads diarrhoea, so ORS and soap should be distributed on arrival. Report any cluster of diarrhoea or fever to the community clinic health worker immediately.
